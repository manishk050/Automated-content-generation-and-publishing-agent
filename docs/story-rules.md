# Story Rules - What Makes a Story Work

These rules were learned from real people's accounts online, not
invented in a prompt. Stories are adapted in original words, never
copied. The real queue and its source links stay private. The demo
story in this repo is fake and written for the demo only.

## The shape every story needs

1. Normal life first. Start with an ordinary day, an ordinary flat,
   an ordinary job. The listener must believe the narrator before
   anything strange happens.
2. One small detail. One thing is slightly wrong: a light, a sound,
   a note, a door. Not five strange things at once.
3. A rule with a cost. The narrator learns a rule, and breaking it
   costs something. A rule with no cost is just advice.
4. One concrete proof at the end. A photo, a note, a missing object,
   a timestamp. The story must end with something the narrator
   can point to, not just a feeling.

If a story has no proof at the end, it is incomplete. Do not use it.

## The monster stays mostly unseen

- Show the effect, not the creature. A light going out, a handle
  turning, a note appearing.
- One clear glimpse at most, late in the story, and only if the
  proof supports it.
- Never describe the same monster in two different ways in one story.

## Voice and point of view

- First person, past tense, told like it happened to the narrator.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- No long backstory, no explaining the lore. The narrator does
  not know what it is, and neither should the listener yet.

## Variety rules

- Never run the same story type twice in a row.
- Countdown stories: one a week at most.
- Door stories: one a week at most.
- Series test: a 4-part story where each part ends on an audience
  vote, and the next part follows the winning vote. One variable
  at a time, judged on views and watch time.

## What to reject

- A story copied word for word from a post.
- A story with no normal life at the start.
- A story with no proof at the end.
- A story where the narrator already knows what the thing is.
- A story that needs gore to be scary. If it only works with gore,
  the story is weak.

## Sourcing, honestly

- Source: real people's accounts online, for example r/nosleep
  and r/letsnotmeet.
- Adapt in original words. Keep the shape and the proof, rewrite
  the sentences.
- Never copy text, never fake a source, never claim a made up
  story is a real account.
